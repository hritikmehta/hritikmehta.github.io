The Problem

From booking confirmation to boarding the flight, most Indian couples receive almost no proactive communication from their travel company. The information exists — nobody is surfacing it at the right time.

User Journey

Six stages, five anxiety spikes

Post-booking isn't one moment — it's a sequence of increasingly stressful stages, each with a distinct trigger, a distinct question, and a distinct opportunity to help.

Anxiety level across the booking-to-travel window
Booking confirmed
Initial relief, then the questions start. "Did I book the right hotel?" "Do I need a visa?"
Visa phase
Documents, VFS appointments, rejection fear. No status updates. Highest ambient anxiety.
T−30 days
Insurance, currency, SIM card, baggage rules. Moderate anxiety, lots of decisions.
T−7 days
Packing anxiety, online check-in, final confirmations. Stress rises again sharply.
Airport day
Peak. Zero margin for error. Terminal questions, missing docs, no WiFi, no time.
On trip
Reduced. Occasional on-ground queries. Mostly enjoying — but emergencies still happen.
03
Root Causes

Four reasons couples feel anxious post-booking

01
Information Gap
"What documents do I need for Bali?"
They don't know what they don't know. The information exists in their booking, but nobody is surfacing it. They Google instead of being told.
02
Status Gap
"Has my visa actually been submitted?"
No visibility into where things stand. They've handed over documents and money, then heard nothing. The absence of bad news doesn't feel like good news.
03
Decision Paralysis
"Do I need travel insurance for this trip?"
Too many decisions, unclear urgency, conflicting information online. They need someone who knows their specific trip to tell them what actually applies.
04
Time Pressure
"I have 48 hours and 3 things still pending"
Urgency without clarity. They're close to departure and unsure if they've done everything. No proactive nudge meant they forgot something obvious.

Feature 01 — Checklist

The right task at the right time

1
Stage detection
Days to departure determine the current stage. T-51 is visa prep. T-7 is packing. T-2 is airport readiness. Each stage has its own task set.
2
Trip-specific tasks
Bali's checklist has VoA (Visa on Arrival) cash instructions. Paris has Schengen appointment reminders. Not a generic list — your list.
3
Up-next focus
One prominent "Up next" card with 2–3 items max. Primary action: Mark Done. The assistant is secondary — we don't want to train users to over-rely on it.
4
Stage transition
When all current-stage items are done (or the date threshold passes), the checklist advances automatically and triggers a push notification.
5
Assistant as fallback
"Ask the assistant" appears as a secondary CTA — available if something in the checklist needs clarification, but never the first thing offered.

When notifications are sent
Stage transition triggered When daysLeft crosses a stage threshold (T−30, T−7, T−2), the checklist resets and a transition notification is sent immediately.
Pending item reminder If any checklist item remains unchecked 24 hours before the stage deadline, a reminder is sent. Not sent if everything is complete.
Departure-day nudge Sent at 8:00 AM on the day before departure. Only sent if items remain open. Silent if everything is done.
Never spammy Maximum one notification per 24 hours per trip. All notifications are deep-linked directly to the relevant checklist item.
Success metric for notifications
Notification-to-action rate % of notifications that result in a checklist item being marked done within 2 hours. Target: >40%.

Simple facts hit Groq 8B in under 200ms. Complex queries use Groq 70B or Claude Haiku. Emergency escalations skip AI entirely — direct human handoff.

After the user's first message, a subtle "Talk to an executive" option appears at the bottom. We surface it — but don't push it. Lower clicks = better chatbot.

Each chat session ends with a 0–5 star rating. Average rating and escalation rate together tell us whether the bot is actually solving problems or just generating responses.

Backend · Evaluation

How we know the bot is telling the truth

Vibes aren't metrics. We run two evaluation passes on every production session: a factual grounding check against trip data, and an LLM judge that scores tone and answer quality independently.

Eval 01 — Routing Accuracy
Labeled test set
200 queries hand-labeled with correct tier. classifyQuery() runs on each; mismatch tracked weekly.
Failure modes
T1→T3 misroute = wasted cost. T3→T1 = poor answer. Escalate misroute = emergency on wrong path. All tracked separately.
When it runs: Weekly on the labeled set. Any tier drop below 85% triggers a keyword rule review before next deploy.
Target: >90% overall · Escalate tier: 100%
Eval 02 — Factual Grounding
Precision
correct facts ÷ facts stated
Of everything the bot asserts, how much is actually in the trip record? Catches hallucination.
Recall
facts surfaced ÷ relevant facts
Of all relevant facts in the record, how many did the bot surface? Catches omission.
How it runs: 50 query–response pairs sampled weekly. A script cross-references each stated fact against the raw trip record. Flags hallucinations and omissions separately.
Target: Precision >98% · Recall >80%
Eval 03 — LLM as Judge
Trip context + user query + bot response
↓
Judge · GPT-4o / Claude Opus "Score each dimension 1–5. Return JSON only."
↓
Tone Accuracy Conciseness Actionability Anxiety reduction
When it runs: All escalated sessions + random 10% sample, nightly. Score <3 on any dimension flags for prompt refinement. Judge never sees the tier.
